I am asking for **recognition** — and, if the Centre is willing, a **short letter of recognition** on Centre letterhead stating that OFF TOUR is an innovation from a former NI3C intern (BBIT, Class of 2024, Africa Nazarene University), for me to share with **Colosseum**, hackathon programme organisers (**hello@colosseum.com**), with **hello@colosseum.com copied** on any official email.

**OFF TOUR** lets a class see a Kenyan park without leaving school. In simulation, an autonomous Clearpath Moose stays on designated tourist self-drive paths at a respectful distance. Pupils turn the camera within safety limits; they do not drive the rover. The same pattern can support ranger patrols pupils do not watch. Sensitive species, including rhinos, stay off any live public feed.

NI3C’s mandate includes collecting and sharing ICT innovations for teaching and learning. Nairobi National Park is a practical first setting inside the capital; other parks would follow only with written permission. Kenya Wildlife Service approval for any real drive is still ahead.

Public tourism and park statistics show heavy gate traffic and many learners who never reach a park. Ministry leadership has named digital windows and student-built tools as part of Kenya’s tourism future. OFF TOUR answers from the **school side**: a supervised session with conservation rules fixed in the design — distance, on-road driving, blurred people, no published rhino locations. A public record on **peaq** (booking held until delivery, signed patrol/sighting data, agreed community share) sits under the simulation; schools would pay through familiar channels such as mobile money when that path is live.

I have also taken **Maritime Anomaly System** to a podium finish at the **NIRU National AI Hackathon** and presented that work at the **3rd Mashariki Cooperation Conference** in Diani (April 2026). OFF TOUR is the education and parks build that follows.
